### 2026-10-28, Step 4: parse with mock AI + validation + unclear rows (summary by Claude)

- **What was built:** "Read orders" now works. The page sends the pasted chats and the Chat date to a new route, `POST /api/parse`. In mock mode the server finds Gemini's saved reply by a fingerprint (a hash) of the chats + date, checks every order, turns them into rows and saves them. The table, cards and totals fill, and the chats fold into the slim bar.
- **Reading the paste** (`src/dates.js`, `src/paste.js`): it now understands the iPhone, Android and web timestamp formats (5 todo tests became real tests). A line without a timestamp still joins the message above it. Over 50 messages or 10,000 characters gives a friendly message instead of a crash, so one AI call stays small and cheap.
- **Checking the AI** (`src/validate.js`): the AI's answer is treated like a stranger's form. Message numbers must exist, dates must be real and near the messages, and status and pickup/delivery must be known words. Anything wrong turns the order amber ("Check: ...") instead of dropping it, so the seller always sees it. A reply that can't be read at all leaves the old rows alone.
- **Why it matters:** from Step 7 a real AI writes this data. The checks are the guard between the AI and a PayPal invoice: a wrong order can't slip into an invoice silently. The friendly messages are normal replies, not errors, so the browser console stays clean.
- **One at a time:** the button is disabled while reading, and the server refuses a second parse while one is running. From Step 7 each parse costs one of 20 free AI calls a day, shared with the judges.
- **Choices logged** (DECISIONS.md, 28 Oct): a "Check the Chat date" warning makes the order unclear (Jasper confirmed: check first, charge second). Reading replaces all rows, so more than 50 messages can't be read in two goes.

### 2026-10-28, Step 5: PayPal sandbox invoices (summary by Claude)

- **What was built:** "Send 7 invoices" makes one PayPal sandbox invoice per clear buyer and sends it without emailing anyone. Each buyer then shows "Invoice sent", the payer link and a Copy button, ready to paste into the chat. A real smoke test made one sandbox invoice for Lina: `INV2-BKCU-NGE3-RZG3-U35Y`, UNPAID, 54.00 MYR.
- **The PayPal client** (`src/paypal.js`): it gets a token (like a day pass) and reuses it until shortly before it expires. It builds each invoice from the menu only: names, prices, currency, and the total counted in sen. Before sending, it checks that PayPal's total matches ours. Its error messages say what PayPal said, never a key or token. It refuses to talk to anything but the sandbox.
- **Never two invoices** (`src/invoices.js`): four guards, like four locks on one door. (1) Only clear, active buyers; the server refuses Priya and Danish itself. (2) The store remembers who already has an invoice. (3) A per-buyer "in progress" lock, so a double-click can't make two. (4) The invoice number CHATCHING-{batch}-{nn}: PayPal refuses a repeat, and then we find that invoice and use it. That last path was proven on the real sandbox, not just the fake.
- **Batches:** reading the chats replaces every row, which would wipe the payer links. So once an invoice exists, Read orders is refused. New batch saves the old batch (rows + links) to a file and starts an empty table.
- **Why it matters:** this is the step where real money could go wrong. Every rule here follows Jasper's "check first, charge second": no AI price, no wrong total, no double invoice, no lost link.
- **Testing without PayPal:** a fake in-memory PayPal (`scripts/fake-paypal.mjs`) copies what the real sandbox did on 25 Oct. So 215 tests and the browser check run in seconds, with no keys and no real invoices.

### 2026-10-28, Step 6: who has paid, cash payments, Reset demo (summary by Claude)

- **What was built:** the table now learns who has paid. A "Refresh status" button asks PayPal, and the page also asks by itself every 20 seconds. When a buyer pays, their row flashes green once and the Paid card counts it. In the drawer, the seller can record a cash or bank-transfer payment. "Reset demo" empties the table to start over. The video storyboard is saved in PLAN.md Section 13.
- **Polling without flooding PayPal:** the page only asks while the tab is visible, an invoice is still open, and for at most 30 minutes. The server remembers PayPal's answer for 10 seconds and lets many tabs share one question. So even many open tabs cost at most a few calls a minute. Like a shop assistant who checks the post once, not once per customer asking.
- **Cash and bank payments:** the seller only says HOW the buyer paid. The amount is the full total of the invoice saved at PayPal, checked against the menu, so it can't be mistyped or come from the AI. Recording twice is refused three ways: our saved status, a lock while recording, and a fresh question to PayPal (a buyer who already paid online is marked Paid instead).
- **A race we closed:** a status check that started before a cash payment could finish after it and turn "Paid (cash/bank)" back into "Sent". Now a final status is never downgraded, with a test for it.
- **Reset demo** never calls PayPal: real invoices stay in the sandbox, and any sent ones are saved to `batches/` first.
- **Real sandbox proof:** the Step 5 invoice for Lina went UNPAID -> MARKED_AS_PAID with one CASH payment of 54.00 MYR.

### 2026-10-29, Step 10b: Kitchen list (summary by Claude)

### 2026-10-29, Step 8: accuracy check (summary by Claude)

- **What was built:** `scripts/accuracy.mjs` answers "how well does the AI read these chats?". It compares an AI reply with a file of correct answers and prints a score per field (buyers, cancellations, items, quantities, dates, unclear flags, pickup/delivery, prices) plus every difference in plain English, e.g. "Jun Wei: expected 2 x Cheese brownies, AI said 1."
- **Why one scorer:** it reuses `src/score-parse.js`, the scorer from the Step 3b spike. Two scorers could quietly disagree, and then "perfect" in Step 7 and "perfect" in Step 8 would mean different things. The scorer's messages became plain English with menu names; it still compares item ids underneath.
- **Two modes:** `--cached` scores the saved reply and costs nothing, so `npm run check` runs it every time. If someone edits the answer key or the demo reply and they stop matching, the check goes red. `--live` makes one real parse through the app's own AI code (main model, then the backup), so it measures what the seller would really get, wait time included.
- **Why the holdout rules:** a score on chats the prompt was tuned on is like marking your own homework. The real chats go in a git-ignored folder (they are real people's messages), are run once on 7 Nov, and the prompt is never changed afterwards because of them. Otherwise the number stops being honest.
- **What the live run showed:** Google answered "high demand" (503) on both models: the main one only after 43 s, close to our 45 s timeout. So there is no live score yet, and nothing was written up as if there were. It also tells Step 9 something useful: a busy Google can keep the seller waiting ~47 s before "AI busy".
- **What testing caught:** the money text used a hidden non-breaking space ("RM 15.00"), which made two identical-looking lines unequal; plain-text output now uses a normal space. Shell quoting garbled a few regexes on the way, and the tests caught every one.

### 2026-10-30, Step 9: time budget, retry, backup, daily counter (summary by Claude)

- **What was built:** one **60-second budget** for a whole "Read orders". The main Gemini model gets up to 35 s; the backup model gets the rest (always at least 25 s). If Google says "busy" (503) or "slow down" (429) quickly, the app waits 2 s and asks once more, but only if a real read (~20 s) still fits. Never more than 3 calls. If nothing works, the seller sees a friendly notice and keeps their rows.
- **Why a budget, not a timeout per call:** before, each call had its own 45 s timeout, so a busy Google could make the seller wait ~47 s, or 90+ s in the worst case, before any news. A budget is like a taxi meter with a fixed cap: however the trip goes, it never costs more than 60 s.
- **Words that tell the truth:** the page asks the server once a second which step it's on, and shows "Reading the chats..." or "The AI is busy, trying the backup...". The words only change when the server really switched models, never on a guess.
- **Daily counter:** Google gives each model about 20 free calls a day, shared by the seller and every judge. The app counts every call it starts (failed ones too, to be safe), saves the count in a file so a restart doesn't reset it, and starts again at midnight Pacific time. At 0 it doesn't call at all and says when to come back. The demo and chats read earlier still work, because they never needed a call.
- **What looking at the clock caught:** "3 PM Malaysia time" is only right until Sunday. US summer time ends on 1 Nov 2026, and from then on midnight Pacific is **4 PM** Malaysia time, for all of judging. So the hour is worked out from the date instead of written in, and a test checks both.
- **Testing without waiting:** the tests use a fake Gemini, a fake clock and a fake timer, so "the main model hangs for 35 s, then the backup answers in 18 s" runs in a millisecond. A browser check (`npm run ui-ai`) answers the fake Gemini by hand, so it can look at the page in the middle of a parse.
- **What the live run showed:** Google was busy again. The main model answered 503 after 24 s (too late for a retry), and the backup gave no answer in its 36 s. The seller would have seen the friendly notice at exactly 60 s, with 2 calls used. So there is still no live score, but the new budget did its job on the first real try.
